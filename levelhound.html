LevelHound

A low-cost, servo-driven robot dog that keeps whatever it carries level, whether it's walking on flat ground or standing on a slope. Built with teammate Ishmael Auqui.

STATUSAll parts printed · assembly underway
TEAM2 students
HARDWAREESP32 · 10 servos · 2 IMUs · load cell
TARGETPayload within 3° of level on 25° slopes

The problem

Robots tilt on uneven ground and slopes, which spills, damages or disturbs delicate payloads like lab samples, sensors and fragile items. Legged robots handle rough terrain better than wheels, but few actively keep their payload level, and the ones that do are expensive research platforms.

The idea

Level it twice. If the legs tilt the body back toward level first, a small two-axis gimbal on top only has to correct the leftover tilt. Our hypothesis is that this combination keeps the payload within 3° of level on slopes up to 25°, which a gimbal alone can't do.

Three behaviors

The robot switches between three states based on what its sensors read: an IMU on the body for tilt, a second IMU on the tray to check the payload, and a load cell under the payload for weight.

A · WalkThe gimbal keeps the payload level while the robot walks.
B · CrouchWhen the load cell reads more than 300 g, the robot lowers itself for stability.
C · LevelOn slopes steeper than 15°, the legs level the body and the gimbal fine-tunes the rest.

Design so far

We moved the frame from laser-cut plywood to a fully 3D-printed chassis, still aiming for under 1.5 kg. Every printed part is now done, and we're assembling the robot. Each leg has two degrees of freedom (hip and knee) on aluminum servo brackets. The gimbal stacks the pitch and roll servos, with a 1 kg load cell under a 3D-printed payload bracket.

SUBSYSTEMCOMPONENTS
Power2S LiPo (7.4 V) → 15 A fuse → switch → 5 V UBEC, feeding the servo rail (with a 1000 µF bulk capacitor) and the ESP32
ControlESP32 on a shared I²C bus (SDA G21, SCL G22)
ActuationPCA9685 16-channel servo driver: channels 0–7 legs, 8 gimbal roll, 9 gimbal pitch
Servos9 × MG996R (legs and roll), 1 × MG90S (pitch)
SensingTwo MPU-6050 IMUs, body (0x68) and tray (0x69); 1 kg load cell with HX711 amplifier (G16/G17)

How we'll test it

An adjustable ramp from 5° to 25° and a cup of water on the tray. The tray IMU built into the robot measures payload tilt directly. We set pass/fail targets before building anything:

TESTTARGET
SlopePayload tilt ≤ 3° from 0–25°, and lower than gimbal-only at 20° and 25°
WalkingPayload tilt ≤ 5° RMS, at least 50% lower than with the gimbal off
Slope modeTriggers within 2 s of passing 15°, with no false triggers over 2 min of flat walking
Crouch modeTriggers correctly above 300 g in at least 9 of 10 trials
SpillA cup filled to 1 cm below the rim loses under 2 g of water on a flat walk and a ramp
